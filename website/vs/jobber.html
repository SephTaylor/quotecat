QuoteCat vs Jobber: who is each one for?

Both apps quote and invoice. They're built for different sizes of business. Here's the actual difference — facts, no hit piece. If Jobber is the right tool for you, the answer below says so.

TL;DR — who picks which

Both apps work. The pick depends on what your business looks like today.

Pick QuoteCat if…

  • You're solo or run a small crew (1-5 people)
  • You want a free tier that's actually free — no trial, no card
  • You want flat pricing, not per-user fees
  • You want real profit margin shown on every quote line
  • You'd rather keep your own Stripe relationship than route payments through a platform
  • You quote and invoice on your phone more than at a desk

Pick Jobber if…

  • You have a crew of 5+ that needs scheduling and dispatch
  • You need a customer self-service portal for status updates
  • You want one platform for quoting + jobs + CRM + invoicing
  • You can budget $50-300+/month for operations software
  • You rely on QuickBooks sync or other deep integrations
  • You'd rather have one bill for software + payment processing

Where each one actually wins

Honest read on the feature scope, not marketing talking points.

QuoteCat wins on

  • Real profit margin shown on every quote line, in real time
  • Pricing Health Check that audits your past quotes for underpriced bids (Pro+)
  • Free tier that's genuinely free, no trial pressure
  • Flat pricing, no per-user fees

Jobber wins on

The model difference (the part nobody usually explains)

Jobber is field service management software. It's built to run a contracting business at the operations level — scheduling, dispatching, customer-facing portal, CRM, integrations. Quoting and invoicing are part of it, but they're features inside a larger platform. That breadth is why it's priced at $50-300+/month per user equivalent.

QuoteCat is a contractor financial intelligence platform. It does quoting and invoicing well — and adds the financial layer most apps skip: real margin per line, overhead-loaded labor rates, Pricing Health Check audits. It doesn't do scheduling or dispatch (yet). If you need those, Jobber is the right tool. If you need to know whether your quotes are actually profitable before you send them — without paying $200/month per user for the privilege — QuoteCat is.

These aren't competing products in the strict sense. They serve different stages of business. A solo contractor in year one almost certainly doesn't need Jobber's full stack. A 12-person crew with multiple jobs running simultaneously probably needs more than QuoteCat offers today.

Common questions

Can I import my Jobber data into QuoteCat?
Not directly today. Most data (clients, quotes, invoices) can be exported from Jobber as CSV and brought into QuoteCat via the pricebook import or by recreating client records. If this becomes important to enough users we'll build a real importer.
Does QuoteCat have scheduling like Jobber?
Not yet. Job scheduling, dispatch, and the customer-facing self-service portal are Jobber's strongest features. QuoteCat focuses on quoting, invoicing, payments, and the financial-intelligence layer. If scheduling is a must-have for your business, Jobber is the better fit.
Does Jobber show profit margin per line?
Jobber surfaces totals and runs job-costing reports after the fact, but it does not show real margin per line at the moment the quote is being built. QuoteCat does — every line shows the actual margin (not just the markup), and lines below your target margin get flagged before you send.
Which one is better for a solo contractor?
QuoteCat. It's purpose-built for solo and small operators — free tier, flat pricing, mobile-first, financial-intelligence features that catch underpricing. Jobber is excellent but overbuilt for one person, and you'd pay the most expensive entry price ($49+/mo) for features designed around a multi-person crew.
Can I use both?
Some contractors do. Use QuoteCat to build the quote and pressure-test the margin, then push the approved quote into Jobber to schedule and dispatch. Not a perfect setup, but it works if you're committed to both tools.
